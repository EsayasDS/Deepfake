ABOUT THE PROJECT

Deepfake Detection

A Computer Vision research project by Debre Birhan University, Department of Data Science.

Deepfake generation techniques have advanced rapidly, creating serious concerns regarding the authenticity of digital media. As manipulated images and videos become increasingly realistic, conventional visual inspection is no longer sufficient for reliable detection.

This system presents a multi-branch deep learning framework that combines three complementary facial representations: raw RGB appearance, Spatial Rich Model (SRM)-based residual noise patterns, and Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)-based frequency information. By integrating both spatial and frequency-domain cues, the system captures semantic facial features as well as subtle manipulation artifacts introduced during face forgery.

RGB Branch

EfficientNetB3 pre-trained on ImageNet extracts high-level semantic facial features including skin texture, lighting inconsistencies, and blending artifacts that betray synthetic generation.

SRM Noise Residuals

Three fixed 5x5 high-pass filters from the Spatial Rich Model suppress semantic content and expose subtle noise fingerprints such as checkerboard artifacts, over-smoothed textures, and GAN generation traces.

DCT Frequency

The Discrete Cosine Transform applied on the Y-channel luminance reveals unnatural frequency distributions. Synthetic images violate the natural 1/f² decay pattern found in real photographs.
